What is a WSN-CPS?

CPS

A system that integrates the cyber and physical worlds to interact with the real world.

WSN-CPS

A CPS integrating physical and cyber components through wireless sensor networks (WSNs).

Role of WSN in CPS

Ensures communication between physical and cyber components for efficient data collection and actuation.

Applications

Systems that need monitoring and control, e.g. environmental management.

WSN vs WSN-CPS

WSN goal

Passive monitoring of the physical environment 👁

WSN-CPS goal

Active control and intervention in the physical environment ⚙

Similarities

• High data volume
• Stored data
• Low-latency processing

Differences (CPS)

• More emphasis on privacy
• More severe energy constraints
• Higher processing complexity

Slide 7
Data management similarities

High data volume

Large amounts of sensor data; frequent sampling → continuous, high-volume streams needing efficient storage & processing.

Low-latency processing

Process with minimal delay.

WSN: tsunami monitoring must detect early warnings fast.

WSN-CPS: even stricter — controlling actuators in real time.

Stored data

For historical analysis, reporting and decision-making; reveals long-term patterns & trends for decision support and prediction.

Slide 8
Data management differences

🔒 Privacy

Higher priority in WSN-CPS: healthcare, autonomous vehicles, personal devices.

→ stricter data handling, storage, transmission protocols

🔋 Energy

More severe in WSN-CPS — real-time decisions & actuation need high-intensity processing.

Essential: compression, aggregation, intelligent routing to prolong battery life

🧠 Processing complexity

WSN-CPS turns raw data into actionable insights (high-level knowledge).

WSN: simple aggregation / statistics.
WSN-CPS: algorithms, ML, reasoning.

Slide 9
Constraints in WSN data management

1 · Energy consumption

Battery-powered, remote locations → energy-efficient designs

2 · Memory & processing

Limited storage and computational capacity

3 · Volume & real-time

Large, continuous data streams from sensors

4 · Data uncertainty

Imprecise data due to environmental factors & technical limits

Comparing WSNs and CPS-WSNs
CriterionWSNsCPS-WSNs
MobilityLimited controlled & uncontrolled mobility, mainly for network coverageControlled & uncontrolled, typically mission-oriented; both sensing and actuating devices can be mobile
IntelligenceData-mining & knowledge discovery detect high-level events of interest (often complex event processing)Extract high-level knowledge from heterogeneous networks; reasoning decides actions for the actuation component
Real-timeImportant only for critical apps, e.g. tsunami monitoringMore important: CPS must react timely and control the physical system; delays not commonly tolerated

Slide 16
Controlled vs uncontrolled mobility

Controlled

MEs autonomously decide paths based on goals (max data, min energy).

→ enhances data gathering in targeted areas

Uncontrolled

Fixed / predictable paths (e.g. public transportation) — not managed by the system.

→ when nodes cannot control their movement
