## 2. tm-find-my-offline.mp4

**Title**

```
How "Find My" Finds a Phone That's Switched Off 📍 #shorts
```

**Description**

```
On many newer phones, switching off isn't completely off: a little battery is kept in reserve so the phone can keep sending a Bluetooth signal for hours. Nearby phones from strangers pick up that signal, attach their own location and upload it, end-to-end encrypted, so only the owner can see where the lost phone is. The person walking past never knows, and the company can't read it either. Apple's Find My and Google's Find My Device networks both work this way.

## 3. tm-airplane-mode.mp4

**Title**

```
Why Do Planes Still Ask for Airplane Mode? ✈️ #shorts
```

**Description**

```
A phone won't bring down a plane. Up at cruising height, a phone can see many cell towers at once and transmits at full power trying to reach them. Those bursts can leak into pilots' headsets as a buzzing noise, at moments when they need to hear air traffic control clearly, and they make it harder for networks on the ground. So airplane mode is a precaution. Some airlines now offer onboard Wi-Fi and even mobile data through a small cell site inside the plane, which keeps phones at low power.

## 4. tm-shazam.mp4

**Title**

```
How Shazam Knows a Song in 3 Seconds 🎵 #shorts
```

**Description**

```
Shazam turns a few seconds of sound into a spectrogram, a picture of which frequencies are loud at each moment. It keeps only the strongest peaks, which survive background noise, giving a constellation of points. Pairs of peaks become fingerprints: two frequencies and the time between them. Those fingerprints are looked up among millions of songs, and when many of them line up with the same time offset in one song, that's the match.

## 5. tm-video-vs-email.mp4

**Title**

```
Why 4K Video Loads Faster Than a 2 MB Email Attachment 🤯 #shorts
```

**Description**

```
Streaming video and email attachments travel very differently. YouTube splits a video into small chunks, serves them from a server close to you (often inside your internet provider), and starts playing after the first few seconds while the rest keeps downloading. An email attachment is re-encoded as text (Base64), which makes it about a third bigger, and it hops through several mail servers that each store and scan it. You can't open it until the whole message has arrived.
